<p>
A great contract should matter.
</p>

<p>
It just shouldn't stay great forever.
</p>

<p>
IceCore allows General Managers to find value through the draft, waivers,
prospect development and long-term roster building.
</p>

<p>
Find a player before everybody else and you should benefit.
</p>

<p>
But if that player becomes one of the best assets in the league,
his salary eventually has to follow.
</p>

<p>
That is why keeper salaries grow.
</p>

<h2>Three Paths</h2>

<p>
When a regular player is protected for another season, his salary follows
one of three paths.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-stat-grid">

  <div class="ic-article-stat">
    <strong>+5</strong>
    <span>Outside Top 100 skaters / Top 10 goaltenders</span>
  </div>

  <div class="ic-article-stat">
    <strong>+10</strong>
    <span>Top 100 skaters / Top 10 goaltenders</span>
  </div>

  <div class="ic-article-stat">
    <strong>+15</strong>
    <span>Top 100 skaters / Top 10 goaltenders · Salary ≤ 20</span>
  </div>

</div>

<p>
Most players simply grow by 5 Coins.
</p>

<p>
A Top 100 skater or Top 10 goaltender grows by 10.
</p>

<p>
And when a qualifying Top 100 skater or Top 10 goaltender is still carrying a salary of 20 Coins or less,
the increase becomes 15.
</p>

<p>
Three numbers.
</p>

<p>
Everything else is just the story of how a player got there.
</p>

<h2>The Normal Keeper</h2>

<p>
Start with a useful established player carrying a 35-Coin salary.
</p>

<p>
He remains productive, but finishes outside the Top 100.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>35</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <span>40</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <span>45</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <span>50</span>
</div>

<p>
Nothing dramatic happens.
</p>

<p>
Every offseason the General Manager pays another 5 Coins to keep him.
</p>

<p>
Eventually the question changes.
</p>

<p>
Not:
</p>

<blockquote>
Is he a good player?
</blockquote>

<p>
But:
</p>

<blockquote>
Is he still worth keeping at this salary?
</blockquote>

<p>
That decision is the point.
</p>

<h2>The Breakout</h2>

<p>
Now imagine a player already carrying a salary of just 1 Coin.
</p>

<p>
Then he breaks out and finishes inside the Top 100 skaters or Top 10 goaltenders.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>1</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>16</strong>
</div>

<p>
The General Manager still has an extraordinary contract.
</p>

<p>
The breakout was discovered early, and IceCore rewards that.
</p>

<p>
But suppose the player does it again.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>16</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>31</strong>
</div>

<p>
His previous salary was still 20 Coins or less, so another qualifying Top 100 skater / Top 10 goaltender season
produces another 15-Coin increase.
</p>

<p>
Now the salary is above the low-cost threshold.
</p>

<p>
If he remains in the Top 100 skaters / Top 10 goaltenders group again:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>31</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>41</strong>
</div>

<p>
The bargain survives.
</p>

<p>
It just doesn't stay frozen forever.
</p>

<h2>The Waiver Discovery</h2>

<p>
Some of the best fantasy stories begin with a player nobody owned.
</p>

<p>
A player entering the IceCore economy without an established salary
can be acquired from waivers at 0 Coins.
</p>

<p>
If he remains outside the Top 100 and you protect him:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>0</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>5</strong>
</div>

<p>
If that waiver discovery explodes into a qualifying Top 100 skater / Top 10 goaltender season:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>0</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>15</strong>
</div>

<p>
There is no 0 → 10 path.
</p>

<p>
A 0-Coin player is automatically inside the low-salary tier, so a
Top 100 finish triggers the 15-Coin increase.
</p>

<p>
You found the player for nothing.
</p>

<p>
You still get the bargain.
</p>

<p>
But now the league knows what you found.
</p>

<h2>The Dropped Contract</h2>

<p>
Releasing a player does not erase his IceCore salary.
</p>

<p>
Imagine a player carrying a 10-Coin salary.
</p>

<p>
His franchise drops him and another General Manager claims him.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>10</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <span>DROPPED</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>10</strong>
</div>

<p>
He does not become a new 0-Coin asset.
</p>

<p>
His established salary follows his IceCore history.
</p>

<p>
If the new General Manager protects him after an ordinary season:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>10</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>15</strong>
</div>

<p>
If he finishes inside the Top 100 skaters or Top 10 goaltenders:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>10</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>25</strong>
</div>

<p>
Changing franchises changes the owner.
</p>

<p>
It does not reset the contract.
</p>

<h2>The Expensive Star</h2>

<p>
Salary growth is not only about cheap players.
</p>

<p>
Imagine an established superstar already carrying a 95-Coin salary
and continuing to finish inside the Top 100 skaters or Top 10 goaltenders.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>95</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <span>105</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <span>115</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>125</strong>
</div>

<p>
There is nothing wrong with keeping him.
</p>

<p>
He may be worth every Coin.
</p>

<p>
But under a 500-Coin Salary Cap, the rest of the franchise has to live
with that decision too.
</p>

<p>
Keeping an elite player is easy.
</p>

<p>
Building around an increasingly expensive elite player is the challenge.
</p>

<h2>The Rookie</h2>

<p>
Then there are players who enter the system differently.
</p>

<p>
Every IceCore Entry Draft selection joins his franchise at:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <strong>1 COIN</strong>
</div>

<p>
There is no immediate salary escalation.
</p>

<p>
He was drafted at 1 Coin.
</p>

<p>
That is his salary.
</p>

<p>
If he eventually becomes a regular player and is protected again in a
future offseason, normal salary escalation begins.
</p>

<p>
Imagine that rookie becomes a Top 100 player before his next regular
keeper decision.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>1</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>16</strong>
</div>

<p>
The franchise receives the benefit of finding and developing the player.
</p>

<p>
Success eventually begins moving the contract.
</p>

<h2>The Prospect Who Stays on the Farm</h2>

<p>
Development Keepers work differently.
</p>

<p>
A player protected from the franchise's NA farm enters the new season
at:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <strong>1 COIN</strong>
</div>

<p>
And if he remains eligible for the development system and is protected
from the NA farm again the following offseason, he can remain:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>1</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>1</strong>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>1</strong>
</div>

<p>
IceCore does not charge a franchise more simply for being patient with
a prospect who is still developing on the farm.
</p>

<p>
The development system is there to reward scouting and patience,
not to create salary pressure before the player becomes part of the
regular roster economy.
</p>

<p>
When that player eventually becomes a regular keeper, the normal salary
rules begin to matter.
</p>

<h2>Cheap Does Not Mean Permanent</h2>

<p>
This is the balance IceCore is trying to create.
</p>

<p>
Find a breakout early?
</p>

<p>
You deserve the advantage.
</p>

<p>
Develop a prospect for years?
</p>

<p>
You deserve the 1-Coin asset.
</p>

<p>
Discover somebody on waivers before the rest of the league?
</p>

<p>
You deserve the bargain.
</p>

<p>
But once those players become established pieces of the franchise,
their contracts begin moving with them.
</p>

<p>
The system does not immediately erase good scouting by forcing every
breakout player to market value.
</p>

<p>
And it does not allow elite production to remain almost free forever.
</p>

<blockquote>
Find value early.<br>
Benefit from it.<br>
Then decide how long it is still worth keeping.
</blockquote>

<h2>The Decision Returns Every Year</h2>

<p>
That is what salary escalation is really for.
</p>

<p>
Not inflation for the sake of inflation.
</p>

<p>
Not punishment for finding a great player.
</p>

<p>
It makes the same question return every offseason:
</p>

<blockquote>
At his new salary, do I still want him?
</blockquote>

<p>
Sometimes the answer will be obvious.
</p>

<p>
Sometimes a 5-Coin increase will be enough to change it.
</p>

<p>
Sometimes a 100-Coin superstar will still be worth every bit of the cap
space he consumes.
</p>

<p>
And sometimes the hardest decision will be releasing a player you found
years ago for almost nothing.
</p>

<p>
That is not the system taking your advantage away.
</p>

<p>
That is the advantage becoming a contract.
</p>

<p>
<strong>Great value should be rewarded.</strong>
</p>

<p>
<strong>Great value should also evolve.</strong>
</p>
