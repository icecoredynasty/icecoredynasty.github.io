<p>
A great contract should matter.
</p>

<p>
It just shouldn't stay great forever.
</p>

<p>
IceCore allows General Managers to find value through the draft, waivers,
prospect development and long-term roster building.
</p>

<p>
Find a player before everybody else and you should benefit.
</p>

<p>
But if that player becomes one of the best assets in the league,
his salary eventually has to follow.
</p>

<p>
That is why keeper salaries grow.
</p>

<h2>Two Paths</h2>

<p>
When a regular player is protected for another season, his salary follows
a simple two-part system.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-stat-grid">
  <div class="ic-article-stat">
    <strong>+4</strong>
    <span>Every regular keeper</span>
  </div>

  <div class="ic-article-stat">
    <strong>+6</strong>
    <span>60 selected Top 150 value players</span>
  </div>
</div>

<p>
Every regular keeper receives a 4-Coin increase.
</p>

<p>
In addition, 60 selected players from the Top 150 skaters receive a 6-Coin
Value Bonus: 20 from ranks 1–50, 20 from ranks 51–100 and 20 from ranks
101–150.
</p>

<p>
The selected players are determined by how many Fan Points they produced
for each Coin of salary during the completed season. The market determines
which contracts represent the best value.
</p>

<p>
A selected Value Bonus player therefore receives a total increase of
10 Coins for that offseason.
</p>

<p>
There are no fixed salary thresholds. The system stays dynamic because the
value of a contract is judged against the actual market salaries and the
production they generated.
</p>
<p>
The current value ranking will be published and updated on the IceCore
homepage, making the system part of the league's ongoing content. Final
Value Bonus recipients are confirmed after the completed season.
</p>

<h2>The Normal Keeper</h2>

<p>
Start with a useful established player carrying a 34-Coin salary.
</p>

<p>
He remains productive, but is not selected for the Value Bonus.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>34</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>38</strong>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>42</strong>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>46</strong>
</div>

<p>
Nothing dramatic happens.
</p>

<p>
Every offseason the General Manager pays another 4 Coins to keep him.
</p>

<p>
Eventually the question changes.
</p>

<p>
Not:
</p>

<blockquote>
Is he a good player?
</blockquote>

<p>
But:
</p>

<blockquote>
Is he still worth keeping at this salary?
</blockquote>

<p>
That decision is the point.
</p>

<h2>The Breakout</h2>

<p>
Now imagine a player already carrying a salary of just 1 Coin.
</p>

<p>
He breaks out and finishes inside the Top 150. More importantly, his contract
turns out to be one of the best-value contracts in his ranking group.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>1</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>11</strong>
</div>

<p>
The regular keeper increase is 4 Coins, and the selected Value Bonus adds
another 6.
</p>

<p>
The player was discovered early, and the franchise benefits from that value.
But the increase is determined by the player's actual production relative
to his salary — not by a commissioner-set salary threshold.
</p>

<p>
If his contract remains excellent value in another completed season, he may
again qualify for the Value Bonus.
</p>

<p>
The market decides.
</p>

<h2>The Waiver Discovery</h2>

<p>
Some of the best fantasy stories begin with a player nobody owned.
</p>

<p>
A player entering the IceCore economy without an established salary
can be acquired from waivers at 0 Coins.
</p>

<p>
If he remains outside the selected Value Bonus group and you protect him:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>0</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>4</strong>
</div>

<p>
If his completed season makes him one of the selected Top 150 value players:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>0</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>10</strong>
</div>

<p>
You found the player for nothing.
</p>

<p>
You still get the bargain — but exceptional value is eventually reflected
in the contract.
</p>

<h2>The Dropped Contract</h2>

<p>
Releasing a player does not erase his IceCore salary.
</p>

<p>
Imagine a player carrying a 10-Coin salary.
</p>

<p>
His franchise drops him and another General Manager claims him.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>10</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <span>DROPPED</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>10</strong>
</div>

<p>
He does not become a new 0-Coin asset.
</p>

<p>
His established salary follows his IceCore history.
</p>

<p>
If the new General Manager protects him after an ordinary season:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>10</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>14</strong>
</div>

<p>
If his completed season also makes him one of the selected Top 150 value
players, the increase is 10 Coins instead:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>10</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>20</strong>
</div>

<p>
Changing franchises changes the owner.
</p>

<p>
It does not reset the contract.
</p>

<h2>The Expensive Star</h2>

<p>
Salary growth is not only about cheap players.
</p>

<p>
Imagine an established superstar already carrying a 94-Coin salary.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>94</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>98</strong>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>102</strong>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>106</strong>
</div>

<p>
The regular increase is always 4 Coins. A high salary does not automatically
create a larger increase, and there are no arbitrary salary cliffs.
</p>

<p>
That player may still be worth every Coin. But under a 500-Coin Salary Cap,
the rest of the franchise has to live with that decision too.
</p>

<p>
Keeping an elite player is easy. Building around an increasingly expensive
elite player is the challenge.
</p>

<h2>The Rookie</h2>

<p>
Then there are players who enter the system differently.
</p>

<p>
Every IceCore Entry Draft selection joins his franchise at:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <strong>1 COIN</strong>
</div>

<p>
There is no immediate salary escalation.
</p>

<p>
He was drafted at 1 Coin.
</p>

<p>
That is his salary.
</p>

<p>
If he eventually becomes a regular player and is protected again in a
future offseason, normal salary escalation begins.
</p>

<p>
Imagine that rookie becomes one of the selected Top 150 value players before
his next regular keeper decision.
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>1</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>11</strong>
</div>

<p>
The franchise receives the benefit of finding and developing the player.
</p>

<p>
Success eventually begins moving the contract.
</p>

<h2>The Prospect Who Stays on the Farm</h2>

<p>
Development Keepers work differently.
</p>

<p>
A player protected from the franchise's NA farm enters the new season
at:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <strong>1 COIN</strong>
</div>

<p>
And if he remains eligible for the development system and is protected
from the NA farm again the following offseason, his salary increases by
1 Coin per offseason:
</p>

<div class="ic-article-flow">
  <span>1</span>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>2</strong>
  <span>→</span>
  <strong>3</strong>
</div>

<p>
The development system still rewards scouting and patience, but a long-term
farm keeper is not permanently frozen at 1 Coin.
</p>

<p>
The development system is there to reward scouting and patience,
not to create salary pressure before the player becomes part of the
regular roster economy.
</p>

<p>
When that player eventually becomes a regular keeper, the normal salary
rules begin to matter.
</p>

<h2>Cheap Does Not Mean Permanent</h2>

<p>
This is the balance IceCore is trying to create.
</p>

<p>
Find a breakout early?
</p>

<p>
You deserve the advantage.
</p>

<p>
Develop a prospect for years?
</p>

<p>
You deserve the 1-Coin asset.
</p>

<p>
Discover somebody on waivers before the rest of the league?
</p>

<p>
You deserve the bargain.
</p>

<p>
But once those players become established pieces of the franchise,
their contracts begin moving with them.
</p>

<p>
The system does not immediately erase good scouting by forcing every
breakout player to market value.
</p>

<p>
And it does not allow elite production to remain almost free forever.
</p>

<blockquote>
Find value early.<br>
Benefit from it.<br>
Then decide how long it is still worth keeping.
</blockquote>

<h2>The Decision Returns Every Year</h2>

<p>
That is what salary escalation is really for.
</p>

<p>
Not inflation for the sake of inflation.
</p>

<p>
Not punishment for finding a great player.
</p>

<p>
It makes the same question return every offseason:
</p>

<blockquote>
At his new salary, do I still want him?
</blockquote>

<p>
Sometimes the answer will be obvious.
</p>

<p>
Sometimes the 4-Coin increase will be enough to change it. Sometimes the
additional Value Bonus will make the decision even harder.
</p>

<p>
Sometimes a 100-Coin superstar will still be worth every bit of the cap
space he consumes.
</p>

<p>
And sometimes the hardest decision will be releasing a player you found
years ago for almost nothing.
</p>

<p>
That is not the system taking your advantage away.
</p>

<p>
That is the advantage becoming a contract.
</p>

<p>
<strong>Great value should be rewarded.</strong>
</p>

<p>
<strong>Great value should also evolve.</strong>
</p>
