Every IceCore franchise begins with the same resource.

**500 Coins.**

What those Coins become is entirely different.

## One Cap. Twenty Strategies.

Coins are IceCore's virtual salary currency.

They do not attempt to recreate NHL contracts.

They create a fantasy economy.

Every player occupying a roster has a value, and every General Manager has to decide where the franchise's resources belong.

Spend heavily on elite talent?

Build a balanced roster?

Search for undervalued players?

Bet on youth before the market catches up?

There is no correct answer.

There is only a 500 Coin limit.

## Price Is Not Value

The best player is not always the best asset.

A superstar at an enormous salary may produce more points.

A breakout player at a fraction of that cost may give his General Manager far more flexibility.

That distinction is where the IceCore economy begins.

**Salary tells you what a player costs.**

**Value tells you what that cost allows you to build around him.**

## The Market Sets the Price

The Salary Cap Draft creates the initial market.

General Managers compete against each other for players using the same finite resource.

Hype matters.

Reputation matters.

Age matters.

Production matters.

And sometimes two managers simply refuse to stop bidding.

That is part of the market.

Overpay today and the contract may become tomorrow's problem.

Find value and the same salary can become a competitive advantage.

## Breakouts Become Expensive

Cheap talent does not remain cheap forever.

Keeper salaries rise between seasons, with larger increases applied to higher-ranked players and additional protection against elite production remaining artificially inexpensive.

A player acquired before his breakout can still become an outstanding asset.

But success eventually costs money.

The system rewards discovery without allowing yesterday's bargain to remain frozen forever.

## Contracts Have Memory

A player's salary belongs to the asset.

Trade him and the salary travels with him.

A disappointing expensive player does not magically become cheap simply because another franchise acquires him.

That creates decisions familiar to real franchise management.

Is the player worth the contract?

Can another organization use him better?

Do you accept the salary because the talent is difficult to replace?

Sometimes the most interesting part of a trade is not the player.

It is the price attached to him.

## Five Hundred

Every franchise receives the same number.

No franchise receives the same roster.

That is the point.

Coins create scarcity.

Scarcity creates choices.

Choices create different organizations.

And over enough seasons, those choices create history.

**500 Coins. Spend them like they matter.**
