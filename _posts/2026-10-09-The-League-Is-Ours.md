# ICECORE DYNASTY — THE LEAGUE IS OURS

## Thank You, GMs

The first weeks of IceCore Dynasty have already shown something that matters more than any projection, ranking or salary table:

**all twenty franchises are active and being managed.**

Thank you to all twenty General Managers for being part of the inaugural season and helping turn IceCore from an idea into a real league.

There has already been plenty of activity: trades are being discussed and completed, and GMs are regularly setting and adjusting their lineups. That is a great sign for a new dynasty league. A special thank you to the managers who create content in the league chat about trades, games and other league news — it adds another layer to the IceCore experience.

Of course, not every GM has the same amount of time available. In a dynasty league, activity does not mean making transactions just for the sake of it. What matters is that every franchise is managed and the league remains active and competitive.

That brings us to our first GM change.

We would like to welcome **Zach**, who is taking over the **Rosewell Greys**.

The franchise was previously managed by **Kenneth**. He really enjoyed the draft and was genuinely interested in IceCore, but his schedule proved too demanding for him to keep the roster updated regularly. After discussing the situation, we agreed that it would be better for both the franchise and the league to have a replacement GM who could give the team more regular attention.

Zach was already on our waiting list and has taken over the Greys very early in the season. Welcome to IceCore, Zach — we look forward to seeing what you do with the franchise.

## IceCore Should Belong to the GMs

One of the ideas behind IceCore from the beginning has been that the league should not simply be a competition run by the Commissioner.

Trades, contracts, player valuations and roster decisions shape the league’s economy and competitive environment. As IceCore develops, the community should have an increasing role in how the league operates.

The long-term goal remains the same:

**the Commissioner should be more of a ghost than a ruler.**

The next step toward that model is a new role:

**Co-Commissioner.**

The Co-Commissioner will be based in North America and help with administration, communication and day-to-day decisions when needed. Having league administration on both sides of the Atlantic should also make communication easier across time zones.

The Co-Commissioner should be someone on a similar wavelength to the Commissioner: someone who understands IceCore’s direction and shares its long-term philosophy.

### How will it work?

**GW3 — Applications**

GMs based in North America who are interested in the role can send a private application during GW3. A short message explaining their interest and how they see the role is enough.

**GW4 — Selection**

During GW4, the Commissioner will select the candidate who seems the best fit for the role and for the direction of IceCore.

The Co-Commissioner should understand the league’s philosophy, approach key decisions in a similar way and, most importantly, be someone the Commissioner can work with over the long term.

The goal is not to create another boss. It is to add another reliable person to the administration and a consistent North American perspective.

---

## League Veto: More Responsibility for GMs

The trade approval system will change in the same direction.

Starting in **GW4**, GMs will have the primary responsibility for trade vetoes.

To block a trade, 7 veto votes will be required — just over one third of the league, a threshold intended to prevent individual disagreements from blocking legitimate trades as a common standard in fantasy leagues.

Most trades should simply go through. Every GM can value players, prospects, contracts and their team’s timeline differently; an unusual trade is not automatically a bad trade.

A veto should be a safeguard, not a way to block a deal simply because another GM would not make the same move.

If there is clear evidence of cheating or deliberate manipulation, the Commissioner can intervene.

The goal is to give GMs more control while keeping a basic safeguard for the league.

If the system is repeatedly abused through unjustified trade vetoes, we reserve the right to revert trade approval authority to the Commissioner.

The Commissioner also retains the right to intervene in exceptional cases involving clear fraud, collusion or deliberate manipulation, including correcting a roster if necessary to protect the integrity of the league.

## Salary Cap: A Compromise for the Future

A hard in-season salary cap was part of the original IceCore plan. The draft was built around a 500-Coin limit, and the intention was to retain a salary-based element during the regular season as well.

The problem was that Yahoo did not support a custom in-season salary cap in a way that IceCore could properly enforce, so the league launched without a hard in-season cap.

Several GMs have since asked about salary rules, and the idea has received positive feedback. From **next season**, we plan to introduce a **600-Coin roster limit** together with a salary floor 250 Coins.

Starting next season, there will be **at least three salary checks per season**. If a team is above 600 Coins at a check, its GM will have **one week** to bring the roster back to 600 or below. The salary floor will also be checked during those three reviews.

Any GM who fails to bring their roster within the 600-Coin salary cap or up to the 250-Coin salary floor within the one-week correction period will receive a formal warning and be given one additional week to comply. A second warning in a season will result in the GM’s removal from the league.

The aim is to prevent teams competing for the title from pushing their salary into extreme territory, while preserving competitive balance and meaningful limits across the league.

This is not meant to create constant monitoring or make every roster move complicated. It is meant to keep salary meaningful and discourage the strongest teams from accumulating value without limits.

## Long-Term Decisions Remain Long-Term Decisions

Another question has been whether major changes to scoring or keeper rules could make the decisions GMs are making now less meaningful in the future.

At present, **we do not plan any changes that would fundamentally alter scoring or the keeper structure**.

There have been suggestions for small scoring adjustments, but we do not plan to make them because of the importance of competition integrity.

No scoring system is inherently bad. Different formats reward different types of roster construction; the key is to understand the scoring and build a team that suits it.

IceCore is a dynasty league. When a GM signs a player, trades a prospect, keeps a contract or builds around a young player, those decisions should matter beyond a single season.

Rules can evolve where necessary, but the core structure must remain stable enough for long-term planning.

**What you build now should matter later.**

## IceCore 2?

Another recurring question is whether there will be an **IceCore 2** next season.

The decision has not been made yet, but the possibility is very real.

Applications and expressions of interest continue to arrive, including from managers willing to wait for a future opening. If enough suitable people are interested, a second league could be the natural next step.

If any current GM knows someone who would make a strong founding member of IceCore 2, they can apply through the IceCore website.

If IceCore 2 happens, the priority will not simply be to fill twenty places. We want to build another group of committed GMs who understand the long-term nature of the format and want to help create the league from the beginning.

## A New Home for IceCore on Reddit

We set up our Reddit space some time ago, but getting the season started took a lot of work. Now that the league is up and running, it feels like the right time to use Reddit for more in-depth discussion.

We want to bring trade talk and league topics there as well, and I’m preparing **content specifically for Reddit**, including polls and other discussions that will not simply repeat what appears on the website or in Yahoo chat.

I already have most GMs’ Reddit usernames in the league database, and Reddit was chosen for a reason: almost everyone in IceCore has an account, and it was also where much of the original recruitment took place.

The aim is to create another place for the community to discuss the league, share opinions and get involved in decisions as IceCore grows.

You can find the link to our Reddit thread at the bottom of the IceCore website, in the footer.

# THE ICECORE TROPHY

## Recognising the Playoff Teams — and the Champion

There is one idea we would very much like to make happen:

**a physical IceCore trophy for the inaugural champion.**

Winning the title should be memorable, but recognition should not be reserved for the champion alone. Making the playoffs in a twenty-team dynasty league is an achievement in itself, and every GM who gets their team there deserves credit.

GMs also receive an individual ranking on the IceCore website, based on results and GM activity. Think of it as an extra way to recognise what managers contribute throughout the season — not just where their teams finish.

We would also like to add more awards or prizes over time, if we find options that work for the league.

Questions have come up about whether IceCore could eventually become a traditional prize league. That remains a separate discussion. With managers spread across different countries and jurisdictions, entry fees and cash prizes bring legal, payment and administrative complications.

A physical trophy is a different idea. We will do everything reasonably possible to make it happen and find a production and delivery method that works for the eventual winner.

The concept is a real IceCore trophy with a commemorative plaque for the inaugural champion. It would not be a major financial prize or change the league’s competitive structure; it would be a tangible reward for winning a long dynasty season.

If we can make it work, the trophy could become an IceCore tradition — a physical piece of league history for each champion. There are still practical details to solve, so this is **a plan we are working toward**. We will nevertheless make every effort to find a solution that works.

Winning the first IceCore Dynasty season should be worth remembering — and reaching the playoffs should be worth celebrating, too.

## What Happens Next

The next steps are taking shape:

- **GW3:** North American GMs can submit applications for the Co-Commissioner role.
- **GW4:** The Commissioner selects the Co-Commissioner, and the new 7-vote trade veto system takes effect.
- **From next season:** the 600-Coin roster limit and salary floor will be introduced, with three salary checks per season.

These changes are intended to give GMs more responsibility while keeping the league stable and competitive.

## The League Keeps Evolving

IceCore was never supposed to stop developing once the original rules were published.

The first draft created the initial economy. The opening weeks are showing us how the rules work in practice, which ideas need refinement and where GMs can take on more responsibility.

The objective is not constant change. It is to improve the league without undermining the long-term decisions that have already been made.

Interestingly, I wrote this article a few days ago, and some of the points in it were already being discussed in the Yahoo chat yesterday. To me, that confirms we are thinking in the right direction: ideas are turning into practical changes, and we are learning from one another as we go.

The framework belongs to IceCore. What happens inside it belongs increasingly to the GMs.

**Build the team. Live with the decisions.**
