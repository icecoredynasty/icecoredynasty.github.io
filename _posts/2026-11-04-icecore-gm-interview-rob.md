Five gameweeks are now in the books, and IceCore Dynasty has its first
team sitting proudly at the top of the standings. Amarillo Downburst,
managed by Rob, has made a perfect 5-0-0 start - making Rob
a fitting first guest for a new IceCore interview series.

One of the things that makes a dynasty league special is the people
behind the teams. Over the coming weeks, we want to get to know our
General Managers a little better: their fantasy backgrounds, the
thinking behind their teams, their plans for the future and what being
part of IceCore means to them. Alongside these interviews, we may also
feature GM reactions to key matchups and other moments from around the
league.

For our first conversation, we caught up with Rob to talk about
Amarillo's strong start, his approach to building a competitive dynasty
team and the long road that made him a devoted Carolina Hurricanes fan.

## A Perfect Start --- Just as Planned?

Rob says he expected Amarillo to get off to a strong start. He came into
draft day with a clear plan, and believes that plan --- combined with a
few in-season trades --- helped round out the roster and give the team
the talent needed to compete right away.

He points to the draft as the biggest factor behind his early success.
In a 20-team league, and particularly in an auction format, he believes
draft day is arguably the most important part of the season. Unlike a
conventional snake draft, an auction gives every manager the opportunity
to pursue any player they want. That makes preparation and having a
specific plan especially important.

## Be Aggressive --- and Don't Be Afraid to Make a Move

After more than 20 years of playing fantasy hockey, Rob says one of the
biggest lessons he has learned is simple: don't be afraid to make a move
or pull the trigger on a trade.

His advice is to be aggressive and act quickly, especially in deeper
leagues. For Rob, building a competitive roster is not just about making
the right picks --- it is also about recognising opportunities and being
willing to act on them.

That approach carries over into his long-term strategy. His first
priority is to compete early, because every win matters. He plans to
keep upgrading the roster for as long as he believes the team can
contend. Once that is no longer realistic, he is ready to sell and
rebuild.

But he has one important condition: if you commit to a rebuild, do it
properly. Rob believes a well-executed fire sale can turn a team around
surprisingly quickly --- potentially in just one season. He says this
approach comes from his long experience running a fantasy baseball
league.

## The Competition at the Top

When asked who he sees as his biggest competition so far, Rob named
Taylor. The two have developed a good friendship, partly because they
think alike and compete with a similar level of aggression.

He also singled out Zach, who has taken over a strong team and impressed
him as a GM. Standik, meanwhile, made a hot start, although Rob expected
his team's early pace to cool off as the season progressed.

With plenty of hockey still to be played, the race is far from decided
--- but it is already clear that Rob is keeping a close eye on the teams
around him.

## Building IceCore Together

Rob feels that IceCore has progressed well so far. He acknowledges that
the league has gone through some growing pains, but sees that as a
normal part of bringing 20 managers together.

As he puts it, getting 20 people to agree on everything is never going
to happen. For him, the key is that everyone can trust one another. That
trust, more than complete agreement on every decision, is what will help
keep the league strong over time.

## Beyond Fantasy Hockey: Baseball, Cars and the Canes

Fantasy sports are not limited to hockey for Rob. He also plays in
long-running fantasy baseball and football leagues. Away from sports, he
works on cars, building Nissan and Infiniti vehicles for a company that
is a leader in that field in the United States.

And then there are the Carolina Hurricanes. Rob has followed the Canes
for a long time, through the highs and the lows: from their first
Stanley Cup 20 years ago, through nine seasons of losing records, to the
successful Rod Brind'Amour era and the joy of seeing the franchise win
its second Cup.

It has been a long and unpredictable journey as a fan --- which makes
the team's success all the more special to him.

## What's Next for Amarillo?

Rob's message is clear: he is here to compete, and he is not planning to
sit still. His approach is built around preparation, aggression in the
trade market and a willingness to change direction when the time comes.

A perfect 5--0--0 start is an excellent foundation, but the real test is
keeping that level of focus across a long dynasty season. For now,
Amarillo Downburst is setting the pace --- and Rob is enjoying the
challenge.
