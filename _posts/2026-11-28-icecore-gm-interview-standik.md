With more than a decade of fantasy hockey experience, Standik of Hartford Arsenal has a clear philosophy: don't rebuild for years in the hope of winning someday --- find a way to compete every season. From late-night draft battles and calculated roster moves to AI-generated content and the recently dissolved Trade Ethics Committee, Standik brings plenty of activity, opinions and personality to IceCore Dynasty.

One of the things that makes a dynasty league special is the people behind the teams. Through this interview series, we want to get to know our General Managers a little better: their fantasy backgrounds, the thinking behind their teams, their plans for the future and what being part of IceCore means to them. Alongside these interviews, we may also feature GM reactions to key matchups and other moments from around the league.

For this conversation, we caught up with Standik to talk about his approach to building a team that can compete year after year, his views on trades and league integrity, his contributions to the IceCore community and the story behind Hartford Arsenal.

## Win Every Year --- Not Just Win Now

IceCore's combination of dynasty management, salary cap considerations and a no-bench format immediately caught Standik's attention. He sees the league as a unique blend of different fantasy approaches, offering multiple paths to success. Interestingly, the no-bench format also takes away one of his usual strengths: managing player usage, planning schedules and maximizing lineup opportunities.

In a league this deep, however, he considers the no-bench system essential to maintaining a meaningful free-agent pool. He also appreciates the ambition behind IceCore's original concept, even if Yahoo's technical limitations prevent some ideas from becoming reality. For now, he hopes the platform's developers will show at least as much activity as the league's commissioner.

His biggest challenge so far was the draft itself. It lasted until 5 a.m., with work waiting at 7. Building a dynasty was apparently easier than surviving the following morning. 😁

When it comes to his overall philosophy, Standik prefers a phrase that goes beyond the traditional *Win Now* mentality.

*"My philosophy is less about 'Win Now' and more about 'Win Every Year.'"*

Rather than spending several seasons collecting prospects and patiently waiting for the future, he prefers continuously refreshing his roster and remaining competitive. Hartford is still a long-term project, but that doesn't mean he intends to sacrifice present opportunities for distant potential.

If a playoff run looks realistic, he won't hesitate to acquire an older player or take on a higher salary to strengthen his team. He believes short-term compromises can often be addressed the following season, without sacrificing the entire future.

This season, his primary objective is to make the playoffs, where anything can happen. His secondary objectives are considerably more personal: beating Rob and Taylor. 😁

## Every Player Has a Price

When evaluating players and potential trades, Standik considers several factors simultaneously: current production, salary, age, potential and the availability of alternatives on the free-agent market.

His approach is firmly rooted in fantasy value rather than reputation. As he puts it, *"A great hockey player isn't necessarily a great fantasy asset."*

He isn't particularly fond of waiting indefinitely for a player's production to recover. However, he does pay close attention to a player's actual role within his NHL team. If the role remains strong and he believes a slump is temporary, patience can still be worthwhile --- Matthew Knies being one example.

On the other hand, if a player is losing his role and relies heavily on offensive production while contributing little in other fantasy categories, Standik is much less willing to wait. In a league where matchups can be decided by only a handful of points, active management and timely streaming can be just as important as the players drafted months earlier.

Despite his analytical approach, there are players he would particularly like to have on his roster. David Pastrnak and Martin Necas are among his personal favourites, although neither has yet found his way to Hartford.

And then there is the one player who apparently doesn't need to be included in any trade discussion.

*"The only truly untouchable player would be Jaromir Jagr, whose NHL career is unfortunately over, although he apparently hasn't quite finished with hockey itself."* 😁

Everyone else, as Standik makes clear, has a price.

## Fair Trades, Unfair Trades and the Fine Line Between Them

Standik's emphasis on fairness and competitive balance has occasionally made him one of the more vocal participants in league trade discussions. But his perspective on trade evaluation is more nuanced than simply comparing player values.

He acknowledges that player valuation is inherently subjective. Without a transparent market in which multiple managers compete for players, determining their true market value is extremely difficult. A trade that appears heavily unbalanced on paper doesn't necessarily mean that the league's integrity has been compromised.

For him, the real line is crossed when a transaction is deliberately designed to unfairly benefit another team or undermine the league itself --- for example, when a manager knowingly dumps valuable players before leaving the league.

That distinction is important. Competitive imbalance and questionable player valuation are not automatically the same thing as an unfair transaction.

And while the league chat has certainly seen its share of discussions on the subject, Standik might have found a new way to approach the issue: with data, rather than verdicts.

## Bringing IceCore to Life

Fantasy hockey, for Standik, extends well beyond roster management. Community, rivalry, communication and entertainment are all part of what makes a league enjoyable.

That philosophy is reflected in his contributions to IceCore, including AI-generated visuals, analytical models, trade evaluations and other creative content.

He is also careful not to treat AI output as unquestionable truth. When developing analytical models, he aims for reproducible results, data-driven conclusions and numerical verification rather than relying on AI's imagination.

Ultimately, he believes a league's quality depends not only on its format, but above all on the engagement of its managers.

And if there is one thing Hartford Arsenal's manager cannot be accused of, it is a lack of activity. Quite the opposite, in fact. 😁

## Electrical Engineering, Four Boys and the Arsenal

Away from fantasy hockey, Standik has spent more than 15 years working in electrical engineering design, combining technical projects with financial responsibilities within his company. He enjoys his work, even if it can be demanding --- so demanding, in fact, that with four boys at home, including two toddlers, work occasionally becomes a form of relaxation. 😁

His sporting background goes back to childhood, when he played football and floorball competitively. These days, his priorities have shifted towards family life and home renovation projects. His sporting activities, meanwhile, have largely moved to the world of fantasy sports and his phone.

His football allegiance is much less complicated. Standik has supported Arsenal since childhood, and next year will mark 30 years as a fan. A major part of that connection was strengthened by Tomáš Rosický, whom he considers the greatest Czech footballer of all time. He believes that without Rosický's unfortunate injury history, the midfielder could have been remembered among the greatest players in world football.

That passion is reflected directly in Hartford Arsenal's name and visual identity. The franchise is essentially Standik's own hockey interpretation of the London club.

## The Trade Ethics Committee Has Been Dissolved

And what about the latest Hartford trade involving K'Andre Miller and Vlasíc? A deal that was already destined to attract a little extra attention in the league chat?

Apparently, the committee responsible for scrutinising Hartford's trades has undergone a significant organisational restructuring.

*"The Hartford Trade Ethics Committee has been dissolved and replaced by the Data Analysis Committee, which is strictly forbidden from drawing conclusions that aren't supported by numbers."* 😁

According to Standik, the trade was offered to him without prior discussion, and he simply accepted it. He had already tried twice to replace Miller through waivers, unsuccessfully. Miller's fantasy production had also improved in recent games.

From Hartford's perspective, the transaction was straightforward: two defensemen with similar fantasy averages at free-agent level, with one additional advantage --- four games instead of two in the upcoming week.

No big fish. No ethical debate. Just simple mathematics.

Whether the newly established Data Analysis Committee will be able to keep the league chat equally quiet remains to be seen. 😁
