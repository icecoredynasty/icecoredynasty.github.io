The preparation is over.

The inaugural IceCore Dynasty Salary Cap Draft is complete.

And for the first time, the twenty IceCore franchises are no longer just
names, logos and plans.

They have players.

They have contracts.

They have decisions to defend.

**The league is officially alive.**

## Twenty GMs. One Draft.

The most important number from draft day is not the highest salary.

It is **20/20**.

All twenty General Managers showed up for the inaugural live draft.

That matters.

These are people from different parts of the world, different time zones
and different fantasy hockey backgrounds who, in many cases, had never
met before IceCore.

The scheduled draft time was not exactly convenient for everyone.

Nobody had to be there.

But all twenty were.

For several hours, twenty General Managers sat down at the same virtual
table and competed for the players they wanted to build their franchises
around.

That is the moment IceCore stopped being a project.

It became a league.

## Four Hundred Players. Ten Thousand Coins.

The inaugural draft placed **400 players** into the twenty franchises.

Every franchise entered with a **500-Coin Salary Cap**, creating a total
league economy of **10,000 Coins**.

By the end of the auction, **9,855 Coins had been spent**.

Only **145 Coins** remained unspent.

That means **98.55% of the entire league economy was allocated during
the draft.**

The average drafted salary was **24.6 Coins**, while the median was **13
Coins**.

The highest winning bid was **136 Coins**, with Macklin Celebrini
becoming the most expensive player in the inaugural IceCore auction.

But the more interesting story is what happened across the entire
market.

## A Market With More Than Two Tiers

One of the most interesting things about the first auction is how
broadly the prices were distributed.

The draft did not simply create a small group of superstars followed by
an immediate collapse into bargain-bin salaries.

There was a substantial middle of the market.

Connor McDavid and Nathan MacKinnon both reached **109 Coins**. Nikita
Kucherov went for **101**, Cale Makar for **106**, Auston Matthews for
**100**, Quinn Hughes for **102**, Jack Hughes for **98** and Nick
Suzuki for **97**.

But players across the next several tiers continued to attract
meaningful salaries.

David Pastrnak went for 90.

Evan Bouchard went for 90.

Lane Hutson went for 90.

Cole Caufield went for 90.

Wyatt Johnston reached 95.

Matthew Schaefer reached 85.

Martin Necas reached 80.

Moritz Seider reached 80.

And the market continued to step down gradually from there.

That produces a much more detailed salary landscape than simply
separating the league into "stars" and "everyone else."

It also gives the IceCore economy something important:

**room for different opinions.**

Two General Managers do not need to value a player the same way.

They only need to decide how much they are willing to pay.

The table is not intended to identify "winners" or "bad contracts."

In fact, one of the reasons to publish it now is to leave the question
open.

Projected production is only one part of dynasty value.

Age, future role, positional scarcity, prospect development, keeper
mechanics and long-term salary all matter.

That is exactly what makes the IceCore market interesting.

## Nobody Walked Away With a Full Wallet

The auction also produced remarkably similar levels of spending across
the league.

**Ten franchises spent 499 or 500 Coins.**

**Fifteen of the twenty franchises spent at least 495 Coins.**

Only five franchises finished more than five Coins below the full
500-Coin budget.

In other words, the inaugural economy was not dominated by a few
franchises refusing to participate while everyone else spent freely.

The money was in play.

The decisions were being made.

And by the end of the night, almost every franchise had committed
essentially its entire draft budget.

## Yes, It Took a While.

There is no point pretending otherwise.

The inaugural IceCore draft was long.

It stretched to roughly five hours.

But that is also part of what we wanted to test.

IceCore begins with a much larger economy than a typical salary-cap
fantasy league: **10,000 Coins across twenty franchises**.

There are 400 player purchases.

Every bid can extend the auction.

And unlike a standard redraft, the price of a player is not simply about
getting through the current season.

The contract becomes part of the franchise's long-term history.

That makes the inaugural draft the most complicated draft IceCore will
ever have to run.

Future drafts should be a very different experience.

Once franchises already have keepers, development players and
established salary histories, the annual draft becomes a much smaller
exercise in filling the remaining roster rather than rebuilding all
twenty teams from zero.

In other words:

**the first draft had to build the economy before future drafts can use
it.**

## The League Is Already Moving

The draft may be finished, but the roster-building phase is not.

Now the first questions begin.

Which contracts will General Managers want to keep for years?

Which players could become valuable trade pieces?

Which salaries will look different after the first month of NHL games?

Which players might have been bought at a premium?

And which players could turn into exceptional long-term value?

Those questions are deliberately being left unanswered for now.

The draft has only just ended.

General Managers still have time to adjust their rosters, work the
waiver market and begin talking trades.

The market has opened.

The first contracts now exist.

The first trade conversations can begin.

## A Place to Ask Questions

The Yahoo league chat is also open and available for the GMs.

It is the best place to discuss individual rule details, Yahoo
mechanics, roster questions and anything that needs clarification during
the early days of the league.

IceCore is complicated by design.

There is no reason for anyone to figure out every mechanic alone.

Ask.

Discuss.

Compare interpretations.

Then get back to building the franchise.

## No Draft Grades --- Not Yet.

There will be no draft winner or loser list in this article.

That would be premature.

The opening rosters are still being adjusted, the waiver market is only
beginning, and preseason projections are still projections.

Most importantly, IceCore is a dynasty league.

The value of a decision made on September 20 may not become obvious on
September 21.

Some decisions may take months.

Some may take years.

So this article is about the draft itself --- not about declaring which
General Manager won it.

That comes later.

## The Next Report

In roughly a week, the first **IceCore preseason projections report**
will take a much closer look at the twenty franchises.

That report will examine the teams using the current projection data,
roster construction, salary allocation and other dynasty-relevant
factors.

By then, the GMs will also have had time to make their first
adjustments.

For now, the draft gets to stand on its own.

Twenty people showed up.

Four hundred players changed hands.

Almost ten thousand Coins were committed.

And the first IceCore contracts now exist.

A month ago, IceCore was still being built.

Yesterday, twenty General Managers built their first teams.

Today, the league is ready to play.

**The League Is Officially Alive.**
