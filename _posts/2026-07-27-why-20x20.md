Twenty franchises.

Twenty active players on every roster.

**20 × 20.**

Four hundred active roster spots.

It is one of the simplest ideas in IceCore — and one of the decisions that changes the league the most.

## Build a Team, Not a Lineup

Traditional fantasy hockey often asks managers to make daily lineup decisions.

Who starts?

Who sits?

Which player has the better schedule tonight?

IceCore asks a different question.

**Who deserves to be on your team?**

Once a General Manager signs a healthy player and his NHL team puts him on the ice, his performance belongs to the franchise.

There is no fantasy bench.

Your roster is your lineup.

That shifts the decision away from predicting individual nights and toward building an organization you trust over time.

## General Manager, Not Coach

The important decisions happen before the puck drops.

Who do you sign?

Who do you release?

Who do you keep?

Who do you trade?

Which player is worth his salary?

Which young player deserves patience?

A General Manager should not need to decide which of his own healthy players is allowed to matter tonight.

He already made the important decision when he gave that player a roster spot.

**Build the roster. Let the players play.**

## Four Hundred Players

With 20 franchises carrying 20 active players each, IceCore creates roughly **400 meaningful active roster spots**.

That changes the player market.

The league cannot stop at superstars and familiar top-six forwards.

Those players matter.

But everybody already knows their names.

The deeper question is what happens after they are gone.

General Managers have reasons to discover defensive defensemen, physical players, second goaltenders, middle-six forwards, specialists, undervalued veterans and young players earning larger NHL roles.

Suddenly the player who would sit untouched in a shallow league may become an important part of somebody's franchise.

Depth turns knowledge into an asset.

## The Obvious Names Are Only the Beginning

In a shallow league, finding a good player may simply mean finding someone everybody already knows is good.

At 20 × 20, that becomes harder.

Four hundred roster spots push General Managers further into the NHL player pool.

A role change matters.

A prospect earning real minutes matters.

A defenseman becoming more involved offensively matters.

A backup goaltender beginning to take starts matters.

A veteran everybody stopped watching may matter again.

The market rewards the GM who notices first.

Not every discovery becomes a star.

It doesn't need to.

Finding useful players where other managers see nothing is part of building a deeper franchise.

## Depth Creates Identity

Deeper rosters also make franchises different.

One organization may build around elite scoring.

Another may find value in defense and physical play.

Another may invest heavily in youth.

Another may construct a deep veteran roster designed to win now.

When hundreds of players are owned, two franchises cannot simply build the same team around a different collection of superstars.

They have to make choices.

Those choices create strengths.

Weaknesses.

Risks.

And eventually identity.

The obvious names are only the beginning.

## Scarcity Creates a Market

Four hundred active players also mean something else.

Useful players are not unlimited.

When a player emerges, twenty General Managers may understand his value — but only one franchise can own him.

When a franchise releases someone useful, the decision creates an opportunity for everybody else.

When injuries arrive, replacing production may require more than selecting the first recognizable name on waivers.

Depth creates scarcity.

Scarcity makes scouting matter.

And scouting gives General Managers another way to create an advantage without simply owning the biggest stars.

## Every Performance Counts

Depth also means accepting risk.

Your second goaltender has a difficult matchup?

He still belongs to your organization.

A physical defenseman takes penalties but blocks everything in sight?

That entire performance matters.

A player has a terrible night?

That belongs to the franchise too.

You cannot build a 20-player roster and then hide from the parts of it you do not like.

That is intentional.

Good General Managers are not rewarded for predicting every individual game.

They are rewarded for building teams they trust to play them.

Twenty franchises.

Twenty active players.

Four hundred opportunities to find value.

**20 × 20.**
